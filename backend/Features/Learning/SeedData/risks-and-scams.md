## Learning goal
Recognise common loss mechanisms and practise checking claims without using real funds.

## Risk comes from several places
Volatility describes price movement, but falling prices are only one way to lose assets. A custodian can fail or restrict withdrawals. A private key can be stolen or lost. A smart contract can contain a flaw. A stablecoin can lose its peg, the relationship it aims to maintain with a reference asset. Even a technically functioning network can carry an asset whose issuer makes false claims.

Common scams include impersonated support, fake login pages, lookalike tokens, giveaway messages demanding an upfront payment, and promises of guaranteed returns. A professional website, a familiar logo or a paid advertisement is not evidence that the underlying offer is trustworthy.

## Example
A message claims to be wallet support and says your account will be locked unless you enter a recovery phrase. The message creates urgency and asks for signing secrets. Instead of following its link, stop the interaction and independently locate the service's established support channel. Do not send a phrase, transfer assets or sign an unfamiliar request to test whether the message is genuine.

## Common misconception
"It was audited, so it cannot fail." Reviews can miss bugs and do not guarantee honest operators, safe upgrades, adequate reserves or future market prices. Likewise, the name stablecoin describes a target, not a guarantee that redemption will always be possible at that value.

## Key takeaways
- Pause when a request combines urgency, secrecy or guaranteed returns.
- Verify the source, exact asset, network and permissions being requested.
- Never share private keys or seed phrases with a person or a website claiming to provide support.
- Practise using CryptoGuide's simulated portfolio. This content is education, not a recommendation to buy an asset.
