## Learning goal
Compare Bitcoin with other crypto networks without treating price or rank as a quality score.

## Different systems, different purposes
Bitcoin is a network for transferring its native asset, bitcoin (BTC), using a public ledger and proof-of-work rules. The network and the asset are related but distinct: an exchange trading BTC is not the Bitcoin network itself.

Altcoin is a broad label for a cryptocurrency other than bitcoin. It groups together very different designs; it is not a technical specification or a statement about quality. Some assets have their own blockchain, while others are tokens on an existing chain.

Ethereum supports smart contracts: programs whose execution and results are checked under the network's rules. Its native coin is ether (ETH). Gas measures execution work, and users pay transaction fees in ETH. A token on Ethereum is not automatically ETH and may carry issuer or contract risks of its own.

## Example
Two fictional assets trade at $1 and $100. If the first has one billion circulating units and the second has one million, their market capitalisations are about $1 billion and $100 million. A lower unit price does not mean an asset is cheaper in an investment sense. Market capitalisation is price multiplied by circulating supply, not cash waiting to be withdrawn.

## Common misconception
"A high ranking or a familiar symbol proves an asset is safe." Symbols can be reused, prices move, and supply estimates can be incomplete. CryptoGuide identifies assets by stable CoinMarketCap IDs rather than treating their changing ranks as identifiers.

## Key takeaways
- Compare purpose, network rules, supply and control, not just unit prices.
- Distinguish a native coin from a token using the same network.
- Smart contracts add capabilities and code risks; they do not guarantee reliable outcomes.
