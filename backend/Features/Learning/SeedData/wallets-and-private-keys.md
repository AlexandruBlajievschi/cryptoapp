## Learning goal
Explain what a wallet controls and distinguish private keys, public keys and seed phrases.

## Keys authorise actions
A private key is secret information used to create digital signatures. A public key lets others verify signatures made by its matching private key. Wallet addresses are network-specific identifiers often derived from public keys; they are not interchangeable across every network.

A wallet helps users manage keys and prepare transactions. Assets remain recorded on the ledger. A watch-only wallet may display balances without having the keys needed to spend them. A hardware wallet is a way to isolate signing keys, but users must still inspect what they authorise.

A seed phrase, sometimes called a recovery phrase, can restore keys in wallets that use a compatible recovery scheme. It is not merely a website password. Anyone obtaining it may be able to control the associated assets. This lesson never asks you to create, enter or share a real phrase.

## Example
Mira sees a balance in two wallet applications after restoring the same fictional account. The assets were not copied between the applications: both show the same ledger, and both may now be able to authorise actions with the recovered keys. More copies of a secret also mean more places from which it could be stolen.

## Common misconception
"Customer support needs my recovery phrase to fix my wallet." A request to reveal a private key or seed phrase is a major warning sign. A legitimate-looking page can still steal secrets or ask for a harmful signature.

## Key takeaways
- Public information helps others verify or receive; private signing material must remain secret.
- Self-custody puts key security and recovery responsibility on the holder.
- Custodial services control keys for users, introducing reliance on that provider.
- CryptoGuide does not connect real wallets, store keys or hold user assets.
