## Learning goal
Explain what cryptocurrency represents and distinguish a coin, a token and its market price.

## The idea
Cryptocurrency is a digital asset whose transfers are authorised using cryptography and recorded under a network's rules. Many networks let independent computers verify those rules instead of relying on one company to maintain every balance. Networks differ in who can participate and who can change the rules.

A coin is the native asset of a blockchain, such as bitcoin on Bitcoin. A token is an asset recorded using a blockchain's token rules or smart contracts, such as a token issued on Ethereum. A token can represent different things; its name alone does not tell you its rights, usefulness or risks.

A transaction requests a change to the ledger, such as moving an amount between addresses. A displayed market price is separate: it reflects trading activity and may differ across venues. Owning a token does not automatically give you company shares, legal ownership of a physical object or a right to receive money.

## Example
Imagine a fictional network with a native coin called LEARN. A club issues a membership token on that network. LEARN and the membership token use the same blockchain, but they serve different purposes. The token's membership rights depend on the club's actual rules, not just on a balance appearing on screen.

## Common misconception
"If an asset uses blockchain, it must have value." Technology does not guarantee demand, a fair price or a trustworthy issuer. A stablecoin aims to track a reference value, but it can also lose that relationship.

## Key takeaways
- Separate the network, the asset and the asset's market price.
- Ask what rights an asset gives its holder and who must honour them.
